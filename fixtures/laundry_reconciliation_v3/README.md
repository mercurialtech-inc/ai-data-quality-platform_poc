The reporting table is generated by executing the actual reporting transformation, not by manually seeding incorrect reporting amounts. ## 3. Design

Ten locations, 20 source/reporting days, 100 payouts. VA serves eight locations, VB two. Both follow the same business definition. The actual reporting transformation consistently uses the wrong refund-date field for VB. Same-day refunds are unaffected even on VB; delayed refunds expose the bug. There are 50 delayed processed refunds (40 correctly allocated, 10 incorrectly allocated), 100 processed same-day refunds and 30 pending refunds. The independent reference calculation gives 180 PASS and 20 FAIL daily revenue observations. Bank reconciliation gives 86 PASS, 12 FAIL and 2 PENDING overall. Pending cases are not failures (86/98 = 87.8% of resolved cases pass). Of 41 payouts with multiple observed deposits, 34 pass and 7 fail. All processor components and processor totals reconcile with the source; intentional bank discrepancies occur at the processor-to-bank checkpoint.

The two-day batching window and flat $2 processor fee are simplifying demo policies, not real vendor contract terms. Trusted bank matching, one vendor per location, positive bank deposits and end-of-day evaluation are intentional scope limits. See `business_policy_and_metadata.md` for the rule definitions. No missing source-to-payout links, manually overwritten report amounts, or unexplained reporting defect branches. The actual operational causes of bank discrepancies are not asserted.
